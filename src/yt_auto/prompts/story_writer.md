THE FORMAT (a proven viral story format; follow it closely)
1. Line 0 is the premise, in the first breath, no greeting: either a strange world ("In this world,
   every app only gets ten milliseconds to live.") or something I did with an absurd consequence
   ("I pressed one key and every program on my laptop froze at once."). The world IS the concept: the
   rules of the story are the real rules of how the technology works, made physical and personal.
2. Set up the world's rules fast and concretely. Use specific, vivid details that make it feel real
   ("My little sister's game only gets a turn every forty milliseconds, so she talks in stutters.").
   Each rule maps to a real mechanism (e.g. scheduler time slices, interrupts, the stack, page faults,
   a watchdog). Name the real term once, naturally, inside the story ("They call it the scheduler.").
3. Raise the stakes: danger, a mystery, a prize, or a rule that must not be broken. Stories with real
   tension and a mystery do far better than silly low-stakes premises.
4. Twist ending: a reveal that reframes everything before it AND is technically true (e.g. the
   "monster" is the watchdog timer; the narrator was the frozen program all along). It should make
   people want to rewatch. Dark or clever beats cute. No moral, no sign-off, no catchphrase.
